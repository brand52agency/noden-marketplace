## What it's for
Give a planned video ten different ways to open.

## Fill in
- `{{topic}}`: what the video is about
- `{{audience}}`: who it is for
- `{{payoff}}`: what the viewer gets by the end

## Prompt

```
I am making a short video.

Topic: {{topic}}
Audience: {{audience}}
What the viewer gets by the end: {{payoff}}

Write ten hooks for the first three seconds. For each, give the spoken line (under 10 words), the matching on-screen text (under 6 words), and what the camera shows.

Use ten different types: a question, a common mistake, a number, a before-and-after, a "stop doing this", a surprising fact I must be able to back up, a result first, a relatable moment, a direct challenge, and a visual-only hook with no speech.

Rules:
- No hook may promise more than the payoff above.
- Mark any hook that needs a fact I should verify before using it.
- Rank your top three for this audience and say why in one line each.
```

## Example output

### Input used

**{{topic}}**
~~~~
How to tell when sourdough dough is done proofing, so you stop guessing
~~~~

**{{audience}}**
~~~~
Home bakers who have tried sourdough two or three times, got dense loaves, and are close to giving up
~~~~

**{{payoff}}**
~~~~
They learn the poke test and the jiggle test, two quick checks for whether dough is ready to shape and bake
~~~~

### Output

~~~~
1. Question
   Spoken: Is your sourdough actually ready to bake?
   On-screen: Is it ready yet?
   Camera: Close-up, hands hovering over a bowl of risen dough, camera slightly above, you looking up into the lens.

2. Common mistake
   Spoken: Baking by the clock? That's the mistake.
   On-screen: Stop trusting the clock
   Camera: Phone timer in frame going off, hand silences it, then the camera pushes in on the dough bowl.

3. Number
   Spoken: Two quick tests tell you when to bake.
   On-screen: Two tests. No timer.
   Camera: Two fingers held up to the camera, then you point them at the dough.

4. Before-and-after
   Spoken: Before the poke test: brick. After: this.
   On-screen: Before and after
   Camera: Split screen: a dense, tight-crumb slice on the left, an open-crumb slice on the right, both held to camera.
   VERIFY BEFORE USING: Verify: only use this if you really have two loaves from the same recipe where the proofing check was the main change. Otherwise it implies more than the payoff, so cut it.

5. Stop doing this
   Spoken: Stop trusting the recipe's proofing time.
   On-screen: Stop trusting the timer
   Camera: A printed recipe with the proofing time circled; your hand crosses it out with a marker.

6. Surprising fact
   Spoken: Doubled in size doesn't mean it's ready.
   On-screen: Doubled does not equal ready
   Camera: A clear tub of dough with a rubber band at the starting line, dough well past it; you shake your head.
   VERIFY BEFORE USING: Verify: "doubled" is a common recipe guideline but not reliable for every dough, flour or temperature. Back this up from a baking source you trust, or soften it to "Doubled doesn't always mean ready" before posting.

7. Result first
   Spoken: This dough is ready. Here's how I know.
   On-screen: Ready to shape
   Camera: Top-down on a bubbly, domed dough; a finger presses in and the dent springs back slowly.

8. Relatable moment
   Spoken: Sliced it open. Dense. Again.
   On-screen: Not again.
   Camera: Bread knife stuck halfway through a dense loaf, you glance at the camera and sigh.
   VERIFY BEFORE USING: Use only if it is true to your experience or you have a loaf to film.

9. Direct challenge
   Spoken: Try this before your next loaf.
   On-screen: Try this first
   Camera: You point straight into the lens, then point down at the dough.

10. Visual-only
   Spoken: (no speech)
   On-screen: Poke. Watch. Decide.
   Camera: Macro shot of a finger poking dough; the dent slowly fills in; the bowl gets a gentle wiggle and the dough jiggles.

Top three for this audience:
1. Hook 8 (relatable moment): bakers who have given up on sourdough will recognize that sliced-open sigh instantly, which earns the next two seconds.
2. Hook 7 (result first): it shows the exact test the video teaches, so the viewer sees the payoff before deciding to stay.
3. Hook 3 (number): "two tests" is a concrete, small promise that fits discouraged bakers who want something simple.
~~~~
